    RING OPERATIONS MANUAL

    1. Compose an address — select six destination glyphs on the input matrix, then the Solyn point-of-origin glyph (marked ◈) to complete the seven-lock sequence. Or pick a verified entry from the Destination Archive.
    2. Initiate dial — the inner ring spins each glyph to the primary lock; nine lugs engage one by one. Unverified coordinates usually fail on the seventh lock.
    3. Conduit open — the breach surge is not survivable; stand clear of the ring. Launch survey probes through the event surface while stability holds.
    4. Disengage — close the conduit manually, or the ring disengages itself when stability is exhausted.
    5. Aperture shield — seal the opening at any time. A sealed ring blocks unscheduled inbound breaches.

    OPERATOR REFERENCE — TERMINAL VII

    MANUAL DIAL

    Select six destination glyphs on the Glyph Input Matrix, in address order, then close the register with the ◈ Solyn point-of-origin glyph — every outbound vector from this terminal resolves through it as the seventh lock. Hit INITIATE DIAL (or Enter) once all seven slots are filled. The inner ring rotates each glyph under the primary lock in turn, alternating direction lock to lock, and one of the nine lock lugs engages on every pass. On the seventh lock the vector resolves: a registered archive address blooms into a breach surge, an unregistered one faults and the ring spins back down to idle.

    FAST ADDRESSING

    SURVEY DIAL fills the register with six unverified glyphs drawn at random — a gambler's throw against the archive, likely to fault on the seventh lock, though the lugs still engage for the attempt. R is the sure bet: it pulls a genuine address straight from the Destination Archive and dials it immediately, register or no register. Clicking any Destination Archive entry loads its address into the register without dialing it.

    ABORT & DISENGAGE

    ABORT, live only while the ring is turning, halts a manual dial mid-sequence and spins the ring back to idle — use it if a locked glyph looks wrong before the seventh lock. Once a conduit is open, DISENGAGE CONDUIT (or Esc) collapses it on command; left alone, conduit stability bleeds down on its own and the ring performs an emergency disengage at zero. Every survey probe launched through the event surface — P, or a click on the open conduit itself — costs stability too, so don't over-probe a fading vector.

    APERTURE SHIELD & UNSCHEDULED BREACHES

    The APERTURE SHIELD (I) seals the ring at any time, outbound or idle. It is the only thing standing between the station and an unscheduled inbound breach — an off-schedule dial-in the station occasionally detects while the ring sits idle. Sealed, the breach blooms uselessly against the shield and dissipates; unsealed, an unregistered inbound conduit opens on its own and has to be watched like any other.
