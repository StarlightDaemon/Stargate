// what this thing is

Before the Receivership, Kessler–Voss Orbital ran SPINDLE — a private point-to-point transit lattice strung between their towers, tethers, and freeports. Executives stepped through apertures like this one the way the rest of us take a stairwell. Then the money stopped, the towers went dark, and the liquidators sold the network for scrap without ever managing to switch it off. Most of the exchange nodes are still out there: flooded, unmanned, running on trickle feeds and refusing to die, listening for handshakes from a company that no longer exists.

Aperture A7-113 was cut out of the Ossian Exchange with plasma saws and floated here on a barge. The crew re-hung it on a chain gantry in the sub-basement — below the waterline, because the ring drinks three hundred kilowatts when it lights and the flood keeps it cool. Three emitter segments didn’t survive the move; the replacements came off a fab bench in the Narrows and the vendor’s pilot diodes are the wrong colour. Nobody has fixed segment E. Segment E is beyond fixing.

Nobody here holds a KV credential. Dialing is forgery: the rig probes a chain of derelict exchanges, answers their challenges with ghosted responses lifted from captured traffic, and pins a route hop by hop before the dead network notices it never actually authenticated anyone. That takes as long as it takes. But an exchange that has accepted you once keeps the session warm for a while — and if you kept the ticket, you can walk straight back in. That’s a hot ticket. That’s the fast way. The crew burns physical ghost keys — stolen auth chips with a captured session on them — for routes worth keeping warm.

// operating notes (taped to the bench)

  • Pick a route off the board. If the tag is struck through, it is struck through for a reason.
  • NEG on the terminal runs the full forgery. Spool → probe → challenge → ghost → pin, per hop. Go make tea.
  • Segment C stalls on cold spool-up. Whack it. (Click the dark bulky segment when the terminal tells you.)
  • Got a ghost key for the route? Seat the chip in the reader and hit RES — ticket replay, no negotiation, aperture in seconds. A full dial also leaves a session ticket cached until the rig powers down.
  • When the lattice pins, lift the cover, pull the bar. The exchange drops the route on session expiry (~2 min) whether you like it or not. CUT severs early.
