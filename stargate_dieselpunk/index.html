What this machine is

The Halvorsen & Krag Type 88 is a resonance alternator: a 96-tonne rotary machine whose carrier wave is generated mechanically. There are no valves in the carrier chain — the frequency of the traction carrier is the speed of the rotor, nothing else. 1,440 pole shoes on a forged rim; five revolutions per second per kilocycle. When the carrier is governed onto a distant station's resonant signature and that station answers, the traction arc strikes across the stator bore and the span is open. Freight, post, and passengers walk through the bore on the gangway. The Board operates eleven such machines; No. 3 has held the overland record (4,140 miles, Kettle Rapids) since 1929.

Standing orders (long form)

  1. Open the fuel cock and hold the air-start lever until the prime mover catches. Watch lubricating-oil pressure; the clutch interlock will refuse below 25 lb.
  2. Post a traffic order from the ledger, or draw the issued crystal for the destination from the channel rack if one exists.
  3. Hand tuning: engage the clutch, bring the governor quadrant to the destination's speed band, then work the vernier wheel until the beat note nulls and the resonance latch takes. Swing the goniometer to the stamped bearing.
  4. Crystal working: seat the crystal in the oven socket. The crystal bridge overrides the governor and slews the machine to grind-exact speed; the selsyn repeater lays the goniometer on the stamped bearing without hand work. This is the approved fast working for scheduled traffic.
  5. Key the call. The coder drum sends the destination's sign; wait for the answering signature on the beat scope.
  6. Close the main contactor. Do not close early: the overload will trip and must be reset at the alarm board.
  7. To shut down: open the contactor, let the arc die, declutch, close the fuel cock.

Why crystal working is faster

A hand tune is honest work: the operator hunts the vernier against the beat note, waits out governor hunting, and swings the goniometer by eye. A ground quartz crystal in a heated oven is a frequency standard better than any human ear — the crystal bridge compares the alternator's own slip frequency against the crystal and drives the governor servo directly, dead-beat, no hunting. Bearings for crystal channels are pre-set on the selsyn transmitter at the exchange. Board circular 1188 (March 1930) authorises crystal working for all scheduled traffic; hand tuning remains mandatory for unscheduled and distressed stations, whose signatures drift.
